What I learned

Describe your design: why these routers, LANs and links, and how you chose the addresses.

I used four routers in a ring (r1 to r4), each with one LAN and one host.Addresses. All addresses come from 10.63.0.0/16,

Which route summarises several LANs? Which networks does it cover, why is it correct, and what else would it attract if you added a network inside its range?

10.63.32.0/22 , 10.63.32.0/24 to 10.63.35.0/24, while the r1-r2 link is up, the more specific /24 routes win. If the link fails, the /22 sends 33 and 35 via r3, which can reach both through r4.: any network added inside 10.63.32.0 to 10.63.35.255 without its own more specific route would be sent to r3. If it wasn’t behind r3, the traffic would be dropped or looped.

When you took a link down, what happened, and why couldn't your routes recover even though another path existed? What would have recovered?

Both ends of the link went down, and removed every route whose next hop was on it.All routes are static. Nothing tells a router that a neighbour or link has failed.Backup static routes on every router. Each gets a second route for the same destination, via the other path, with a higher metric
